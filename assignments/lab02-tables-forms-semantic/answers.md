## Q1. In your form, why must a `<label>`'s `for` match its `<input>`'s `id`? What goes wrong if they don't match?

Your answer: Matching these two attributes links the text label to its input field so that clicking the label text automatically places the cursor focus inside the corresponding box. If they do not match, the form becomes more difficult to use on small screens and digital screen.

## Q2. You used both `<section>` and `<article>`. What is the difference? Why did the hackathon story become an `<article>`?

Your answer: A `<section>` element is a generic container used to group related thematic content together in a webpage, while an `<article>` is meant for a completely self-contained piece of content that makes sense entirely on its own. The hackathon story became an `<article>` because it functions as an independent news story piece that could theoretically be distributed or republished outside of this website layout.

## Q3. What is the difference between `<th>` and `<td>`? Why should a table never be used to lay out a whole page?

Your answer: A `<th>` element is a semantic header cell that automatically formats text to bold and centered to label a row or column, while a `<td>` cell simply holds standard data entries. Tables should never be used to structure a whole page layout because they completely break layout flexibility on responsive mobile screens.
