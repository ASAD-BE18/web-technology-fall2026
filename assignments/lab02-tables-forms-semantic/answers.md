## Q1. In your form, why must a `<label>`'s `for` match its `<input>`'s `id`? What goes wrong if they don't match?

Your answer:

The `for` attribute of a label must match the `id` of its input so that the label is connected to the correct input field. When a user clicks the label, the related input gets focus, and if they do not match, this connection will not work correctly.

## Q2. You used both `<section>` and `<article>`. What is the difference? Why did the hackathon story become an `<article>`?

Your answer:

A `<section>` groups related content on a webpage under a common heading, while an `<article>` contains independent content that can make sense on its own. The hackathon story became an `<article>` because it is a separate piece of content that can be read and understood independently.

## Q3. What is the difference between `<th>` and `<td>`? Why should a table never be used to lay out a whole page?

Your answer:

The `<th>` tag defines a header cell in a table, while the `<td>` tag defines a regular data cell. Tables should be used to organize tabular data, not to design an entire webpage, because this makes the page less accessible and harder to maintain.
