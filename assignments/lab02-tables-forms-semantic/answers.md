## Q1. In your form, why must a `<label>`'s `for` match its `<input>`'s `id`? What goes wrong if they don't match?

Your answer: The for attribute of a label should match the id of its input so the browser knows which input belongs to that label. If they don't match, clicking the label will not focus the correct input field.

## Q2. You used both `<section>` and `<article>`. What is the difference? Why did the hackathon story become an `<article>`?

Your answer: A <section> is used to group related content together, like the timetable and registration form on my webpage. An <article> is used for a complete piece of content that can stand on its own, so I used it for the hackathon story because it describes a separate event.

## Q3. What is the difference between `<th>` and `<td>`? Why should a table never be used to lay out a whole page?

Your answer: The <th> tag is used for table headings, such as Day, Time, and Activity, while the <td> tag is used for the actual data in the table. Tables should be used to display information in rows and columns, not to design the whole webpage, because semantic tags like <header>, <main>, and <footer> make the structure easier to understand and more accessible.
