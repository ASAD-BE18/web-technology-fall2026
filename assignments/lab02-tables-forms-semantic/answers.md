## Q1. In your form, why must a `<label>`'s `for` match its `<input>`'s `id`? What goes wrong if they don't match?

Your answer:The for attribute of a <label> must match the id of its <input> so that the label is connected to the correct input field. When a user clicks the label, the cursor moves to the input field. If they do not match, the label will not work correctly, and accessibility for screen readers may be affected

## Q2. You used both `<section>` and `<article>`. What is the difference? Why did the hackathon story become an `<article>`?

Your answer:The <section> tag groups related content into a section of a webpage, usually with a heading. The <article> tag is used for independent content that can stand on its own. The hackathon story became an <article> because it is a separate news story that can be understood independently of the rest of the webpage.

## Q3. What is the difference between `<th>` and `<td>`? Why should a table never be used to lay out a whole page?

Your answer:The <th> tag defines a table heading, while the <td> tag defines a regular data cell. A table should not be used to lay out a whole page because tables are meant for displaying tabular data, not arranging webpage content. Using tables for layout makes the page less accessible and harder to maintain
