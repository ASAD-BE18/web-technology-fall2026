## Q1. In your form, why must a `<label>`'s `for` match its `<input>`'s `id`? What goes wrong if they don't match?

Your answer:The for attribute connects a label with its input field through the same id. When we click the label, the related input gets selected. If they do not match, the label may not work correctly.

## Q2. You used both `<section>` and `<article>`. What is the difference? Why did the hackathon story become an `<article>`?

Your answer: A <section> groups related content under a common heading. An <article> contains independent content that can make sense on its own. The hackathon story is an article because it describes a separate event.

## Q3. What is the difference between `<th>` and `<td>`? Why should a table never be used to lay out a whole page?

Your answer: The <th> tag defines a heading cell in a table, while <td> defines a normal data cell. Tables should be used for organized data, not for designing a whole page. Semantic HTML elements make the page structure clearer and easier to understand.
