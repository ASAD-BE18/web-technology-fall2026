## Q1. In your form, why must a `<label>`'s `for` match its `<input>`'s `id`? What goes wrong if they don't match?

Your answer:
The for attribute connects a label to an input by matching the input's id. When both values match, clicking on the label moves the cursor to the input field. If they do not match, the label will not work correctly, and using the form may become difficult for some users.

## Q2. You used both `<section>` and `<article>`. What is the difference? Why did the hackathon story become an `<article>`?

Your answer:
A <section> groups related content under a common topic or heading. An <article> is used for independent content that can make sense on its own, such as a news story or blog post. The hackathon story is an <article> because it contains a complete story that readers can understand independently.
## Q3. What is the difference between `<th>` and `<td>`? Why should a table never be used to lay out a whole page?

Your answer:
The <th> tag defines a heading cell in a table, while the <td> tag contains the actual data. Tables should be used to display structured information, such as a timetable, rather than to design an entire webpage. Using tables for page layout makes the HTML less meaningful and can cause accessibility and responsive design problems.
