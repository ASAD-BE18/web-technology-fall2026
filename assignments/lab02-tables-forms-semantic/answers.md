## Q1. In your form, why must a `<label>`'s `for` match its `<input>`'s `id`? What goes wrong if they don't match?

Your answer:
The for in the label has to be the same as the id of the input, because that is how the browser knows which label goes with which box. If they are different, the label is not linked to anything, so clicking on the label does nothing and screen readers can't tell the user what the box is for.

## Q2. You used both `<section>` and `<article>`. What is the difference? Why did the hackathon story become an `<article>`?

Your answer:
A section is just a part of the page, like the timetable or the join form, and it usually has its own heading. An article is a full piece of content that can stand alone. I used article for the hackathon story because it is a complete news item, and it would still make sense if someone copied it to a different page.

## Q3. What is the difference between `<th>` and `<td>`? Why should a table never be used to lay out a whole page?

Your answer:
th is for the heading cells at the top, like Day, Time and Activity, and td is for the normal data inside the table. Tables should not be used to lay out a whole page because they are made for showing data, not for design, and they make the page confusing for screen readers and hard to change later.
