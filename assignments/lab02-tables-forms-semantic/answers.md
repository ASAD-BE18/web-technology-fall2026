## Q1. In your form, why must a <label>'s 'for' match its <input>'s 'id'? What goes wrong if they don't match?

Your answer:
The 'for' attribute in a label must match the id of its input so the browser knows which input the label belongs to. If they do not match, clicking the label text will not focus the input box, making the form harder to use for keyboard and screen reader users.

## Q2. You used both <section> and <article>. What is the difference? Why did the hackathon story become an <article>?

Your answer:
A section groups related content together under a common topic on the webpage, while an article contains completely independent content that makes sense on its own. The hackathon story became an article because it is a standalone piece of news that could be shared or read separately.

## Q3. What is the difference between <th> and <td>? Why should a table never be used to lay out a whole page?

Your answer:
The th element defines a bold header cell in a table, while the td element defines a regular data cell. Tables should never be used to lay out a whole page because they are meant only for tabular data, and using them for layouts makes the HTML structure messy and less accessible.
