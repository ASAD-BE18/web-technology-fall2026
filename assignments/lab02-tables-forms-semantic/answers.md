## Q1. In your form, why must a `<label>`'s `for` match its `<input>`'s `id`? What goes wrong if they don't match?

Your answer: The label's `for` attribute must match the input's `id` so the browser knows which input belongs to that label. If they do not match, clicking the label will not focus the correct input.

## Q2. You used both `<section>` and `<article>`. What is the difference? Why did the hackathon story become an `<article>`?

Your answer: A `<section>` groups related content together under a heading, while an `<article>` contains independent content. The hackathon story uses an `<article>` because it is a complete story that can be understood on its own.

## Q3. What is the difference between `<th>` and `<td>`? Why should a table never be used to lay out a whole page?

Your answer: The `<th>` tag creates a heading cell in a table, while the `<td>` tag contains regular data. Tables should display information in rows and columns, not arrange a whole webpage, because semantic HTML makes the page easier to understand and maintain.
